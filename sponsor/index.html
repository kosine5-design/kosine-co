The classroom gave me the career

My mother believed in education more than anything. She passed after my sophomore year of college. I finished at Columbia College Chicago with a 4.0, and that fall, at 23, I walked back into the building as a professor.

Teaching is where my career actually started. Moderating a music industry panel at Columbia, I met Cory Llewellyn, CL, who was working with Mims. That conversation became my first real look: co-executive producing Mims's album Guilt and producing its lead single, "Move (If You Wanna)." From there the work took me to Hawaii with Kanye West on a John Legend project, and years later I stood beside Kanye at one of the early Sunday Service gatherings with my saxophone, my friend Phil on piano. The horn came from church in Maywood, Illinois. The classroom came from my mother.

Nineteen years after I graduated, Columbia gave me an honorary Doctor of Arts degree and I delivered the 2025 commencement address. In between, about twenty years of teaching, mentoring, writing curriculum, and masterclasses, alongside records for Rihanna, Justin Bieber, Tank, and Kelly Rowland. The two things were never separate.

One story. Sir Michael Rocks sat in my Columbia class while The Cool Kids were getting started. I opened my mother's basement in Broadview to the group, and we worked there: mentoring, collaborating, co-producing. We put out "Colors," featuring Q Billah. Now, 20 years later, we celebrate the power of mentorship.

What the free Tuesday class is

Internz Academy is a free class I teach on Zoom every Tuesday to people across the country. Mostly adults, some still in their late teens and early twenties, all of them serious about the business and the craft of music. Your support keeps it free: it pays for teaching time, preparation, and the mentorship between sessions, and it keeps me working as an active producer so what I bring to the room on Tuesday is current. Sit in on one.
