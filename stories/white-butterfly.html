White Butterfly and Mama Pearl

A song born from grief, lineage, and the white butterfly that appeared after Mama Pearl's passing.

Her name was Pearl. Panamanian. A dialysis nurse at Loyola who spent her working life keeping other people alive, with a pastoral degree she earned because faith was not a Sunday activity in her house, it was the architecture. She raised her son in Maywood and Broadview, west of Chicago, and she gave him one instruction that organized everything after it.

"My job as a parent is done when you graduate."

She passed in 2003, when her son was twenty. Three years before the day she was waiting for. He finished anyway, because the instruction did not expire. He graduated on Mother's Day, May 14, 2006, with a 4.0, and that fall the school that gave him his degree hired him to teach in it, a professor at twenty-three. Two years later his records reached the Billboard charts. In 2015 came a Grammy nomination. Every credit on this site sits downstream of one woman's promise about her son.

"My mom did the heavy lifting," is how Kosine says it. The Olympic father jumps off the page. The nurse who worked doubles and raised a producer gets this page, because the house writes the second history too.

The Sign

After her passing, a white butterfly appeared. The family read it the way families who pray read such things: not as coincidence, but as a visit. The image stayed. Grief became a melody carried privately for years before it became a record, and when the song was finally finished, there was never a question about its name.

"White Butterfly" was released on August 1, 2026. The artwork carries her: white wings edged in gold on black, light refusing to be quiet. The song is a dedication, and it carries more than one name. There are others this record honors, loved and lost along the way, and their stories will be told here in their families' time.

This is what the house means by legacy. Not marble. A nurse from Panama whose instruction outlived her, and a son who turned the sign she sent into something you can play.
